LeBron James Signs With the Philadelphia 76ers on a Two-Year, $8 Million Deal

James called it his "last decision," taking a steep pay cut from the Lakers to chase one more championship alongside Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey and newly acquired Jaylen Brown.

Leonardo Beltran·July 29, 2026·Updated September 6, 2026·7 min
Correction: Corrected Sep 6, 2026: added the verified signing dates, contract terms, exact quotes and sourcing (NBA.com, NBC Sports Philadelphia, ESPN); the original draft had none of these and could not be verified as published. Re-verified Sep 6, 2026 and reindexed after confirming the signing with two independent primary sources.

LeBron James has officially signed with the Philadelphia 76ers on a two-year, $8 million contract with a player option for the second season, according to NBA.com and NBC Sports Philadelphia. James announced the decision himself on social media on July 24, 2026; within moments, ESPN's Shams Charania reported that Klutch Sports Group CEO Rich Paul, James's longtime agent, had confirmed the same terms to him. The signing became official on July 27, 2026, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. The four-time NBA champion and the league's all-time leading scorer opted out of the final year of his contract with the Los Angeles Lakers, where he earned roughly $53 million in the 2025-26 season, to take the sharply reduced salary in Philadelphia.

Why He Chose Philadelphia

James, 41, who turns 42 on December 30, said he seriously considered retirement before deciding to keep playing. "This is my last decision. I'm not going for money. I'm not going for family," he wrote in the statement announcing his decision, before adding: "I still want to sacrifice. I still want to work. I still want to grind. I still want to compete, to win and to have a chance at the feeling of winning another championship." The same statement, carried by both NBC Sports Philadelphia and NBA.com, continued: "I believe I can help make the Philadelphia 76ers a championship team and I am so excited to energize a new fan base and start this incredible journey one last time."

NBA.com reported that James trained with Sixers guard Tyrese Maxey during the offseason and had competed alongside center Joel Embiid on the U.S. national team at the 2024 Paris Olympics. He also has a personal connection to the team's new President of Basketball Operations, Mike Gansey, who played against James in Ohio high school basketball.

A Roster Reshaped Weeks Before James Signed

Philadelphia's pitch to James was strengthened by a trade completed just weeks earlier: the 76ers acquired Boston Celtics forward Jaylen Brown, the 2024 NBA Finals MVP, in exchange for Paul George and draft picks, according to ESPN. NBC Sports Philadelphia reported that Maxey, Embiid and Brown all took an active role in recruiting James. The projected starting lineup for the 2026-27 season is Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, Brown, James and Embiid.

LeBron James's Team Changes, in Context

  • 2003-2010: Cleveland Cavaliers — drafted No. 1 overall out of high school.
  • 2010-2014: Miami Heat — won back-to-back championships in 2012 and 2013.
  • 2014-2018: Cleveland Cavaliers — delivered the franchise's only championship, in 2016.
  • 2018-2025: Los Angeles Lakers — won a fourth championship in the 2020 NBA bubble.
  • 2026-present: Philadelphia 76ers — his fourth franchise, on a contract worth a fraction of his recent salaries, entering his 24th NBA season.

What Comes Next

Philadelphia has not won an NBA championship since 1983. James will enter the season as the league's oldest active player and is on track for his 22nd consecutive All-Star Game appearance if selected again, per NBA.com.
