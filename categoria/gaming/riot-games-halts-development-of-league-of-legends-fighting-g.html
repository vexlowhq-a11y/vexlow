Riot Games Halts Development of League of Legends Fighting Game 2XKO

Riot Games announces it is winding down development on 2XKO, the free-to-play fighting game set in the League of Legends universe, citing low player retention as a key factor.

Leonardo Beltran·August 21, 2026·Updated September 10, 2026·7 min
Correction: Verificado 2026-09-10: el cese del desarrollo activo de 2XKO para fines de 2026 y el motivo declarado por Riot (retencion de jugadores insuficiente) fueron corroborados de forma independiente ademas de la fuente original.

Riot Games (official 2XKO promotional art)

Riot Games, the renowned developer behind the globally popular League of Legends franchise, has decided to end development on its fighting game 2XKO. This announcement comes less than a year after the game’s launch, revealing that the company has not experienced the necessary player engagement to maintain the game's long-term sustainability. Active development will cease by the end of 2026, marking a significant pivot for Riot as it reassesses its strategy in expanding its gaming portfolio beyond traditional genre offerings.

2XKO was introduced as a free-to-play fighting game designed to attract both League of Legends veterans and newcomers alike. Set in the well-established universe of Runeterra, where League of Legends takes place, the game aimed to leverage the rich lore and diverse character roster from the original title. However, Riot has reported that initial player retention metrics have fallen short of expectations, prompting the decision to scale back on the project.

Understanding Player Retention Challenges

Player retention is a critical metric in the gaming industry, particularly for free-to-play titles. Games of this nature rely on a steady influx of active players to monetize their services through in-game purchases, cosmetics, and other expansions. Riot's acknowledgment of insufficient player stickiness raises intriguing questions about the viability of fighting games in the current market landscape. Despite strong initial interest often generated by big-name franchises, such titles frequently struggle to maintain a player base after the initial launch period. Factors such as gameplay depth, the balance of competitive elements, and community support all play roles in long-term engagement.

The competitive fighting genre has shifted significantly over the past decade, with franchises like Street Fighter, Tekken, and Super Smash Bros. consistently leading the pack. These established titles have built dedicated communities through robust competitive scenes, regular updates, and engaging events. In contrast, 2XKO’s struggles indicate that merely using a recognizable IP may not be enough to succeed in this saturated market.

Implications for Riot's Broader Strategy

Riot's decision to halt development on 2XKO reflects a larger trend within the company. Recently, Riot has been experimenting with various genres outside its primary multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) roots, including team-based shooters and card games. Games like VALORANT and Legends of Runeterra have seen greater success, showing that while the company is adept at crafting engaging experiences, not every foray into a new genre will yield positive results.

The winding down of 2XKO does not necessarily signal a retreat from the fighting game genre altogether. It may instead lead to a reassessment of how Riot approaches this space in the future, taking into account lessons learned from 2XKO's reception. This could include developing stronger community engagement strategies or reimagining how core gameplay mechanics fit within a League of Legends context.

The Future of League of Legends and Fighting Games

As the gaming landscape continues to evolve, the challenges faced by 2XKO could serve as a case study for other developers looking to blend games with established IPs. Riot's pivot may even influence similar projects within the industry, as the firm looks to hone its focus on projects that can sustain both player interest and community involvement.

For fans of League of Legends and fighting games, this winding down also raises questions about future titles within this genre. Will we see other studios step into the arena with new experiences, or does this suggest that the League of Legends universe may not translate as seamlessly into different gaming formats as anticipated? 2XKO's closure might inspire a more nuanced understanding of what players want from a fighting game experience in recognized universes.

As 2026 approaches, speculation about Riot's next move will build. Whether the company attempts another fighting game or decides to channel resources into other titles within its successful franchises, the outcome of 2XKO serves as a cautionary tale about the complexities of genre innovation in a marketplace driven by player loyalty and engagement.
