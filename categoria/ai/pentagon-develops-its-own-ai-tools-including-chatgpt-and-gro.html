Pentagon Adds ChatGPT and Grok to Its GenAI.mil Platform

The Department of Defense has added OpenAI's ChatGPT and a government-tailored Grok variant to GenAI.mil, its secure generative-AI platform, while Anthropic's Claude remains absent following a dispute over military-use restrictions and continuing litigation.

Leonardo Beltran·August 31, 2026·Updated September 7, 2026·7 min

What Was Added

The Department of Defense, which currently uses "Department of War" as a secondary title under a September 2025 executive order (the statutory name set by Congress remains "Department of Defense"), has added two new tools to GenAI.mil, its internal generative-AI platform: a customized version of OpenAI's ChatGPT and a government-tailored variant of Grok. Both are commercial AI models made available to military and civilian personnel through government-specific access programs; the Department did not build these systems itself.

According to the Department's August 31, 2026 announcement, GenAI.mil had already onboarded more than 1.7 million users out of the Department's roughly 3 million military members, civilian employees and contractors. Google Cloud's Gemini for Government was the first commercial AI product to go live on the platform; ChatGPT and Grok are the latest additions.

Who Builds These Tools

The ChatGPT variant is developed by OpenAI and offered through its "OpenAI for Government" program. Joe Larson, OpenAI's vice president and head of government, said the integration is "grounded in [the company's] national security principles," according to defense-industry publication DefenseScoop.

The Grok variant is offered as Starshield AI’s Grok for Government and is based on the Grok model developed by xAI. The Department’s announcement does not explain the relationship between Starshield AI and xAI.

The Department's Claims

The Department's August 31 announcement specifically describes Starshield AI's Grok for Government -- not the ChatGPT variant -- as "accredited for Controlled Unclassified Information at Impact Level 5" and "engineered for secure, consistent enterprise use." It attributes to that tool "immediate productivity gains, stronger knowledge continuity and more secure and efficient collaboration," and highlights capabilities such as adaptive reasoning modes, customizable workspaces and reusable "playbooks." These are the Department's own characterizations of Grok for Government's benefits; the release does not make equivalent claims about ChatGPT, and none of these claims have been independently verified here.

The Anthropic Exception

Notably absent from GenAI.mil is Anthropic's Claude. According to TechCrunch, the exclusion traces back to a dispute over military-use restrictions: Anthropic declined to remove safeguards that would have allowed the Pentagon to use Claude for fully autonomous weapons systems and mass surveillance of American citizens, a characterization the Pentagon has disputed, saying it intends to use AI models only in lawful ways.

On February 27, 2026, President Trump ordered every federal agency to immediately stop using Anthropic's technology, posting on Truth Social that "we don't need it, we don't want it, and will not do business with them again." The Department of Defense was given a six-month phase-out period. The same day, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth directed the Department to begin the process of designating Anthropic a "Supply-Chain Risk to National Security" -- a formal designation that had not yet been completed. Hegseth formalized that designation on March 3, 2026; Anthropic received the designation letter, dated March 3, on March 4. The formal designation excluded Anthropic from supplying products or services for covered national-security systems. A separate Hegseth directive sought to prevent defense contractors from conducting any commercial activity with Anthropic, including business unrelated to military contracts.

Anthropic sued over the designation. On August 27, 2026, U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled that the Pentagon's measures against Anthropic were unlawful, finding them to be unlawful retaliation in violation of the First Amendment, arbitrary and capricious under administrative law, and a denial of due process. "Though the Department of War is undisputedly free to select the AI vendor of its choice," Judge Lin wrote, "the evidence demonstrates that the broad measures imposed on Anthropic were illegal and baseless." That ruling resolved one of two lawsuits Anthropic filed in March 2026; a second case, filed in Washington, D.C., remains ongoing, and it is not yet clear whether the Pentagon will appeal the California ruling or restore Anthropic's access to GenAI.mil.

Timeline

  • February 27, 2026: President Trump orders federal agencies to stop using Anthropic's technology; Hegseth directs the Department to begin designating Anthropic a "Supply-Chain Risk to National Security."
  • March 3, 2026: Hegseth formalizes the Supply-Chain Risk designation.
  • March 4, 2026: Anthropic receives the designation letter, dated March 3.
  • March 2026: Anthropic files two lawsuits challenging the designation, one in California and one in Washington, D.C.
  • August 27, 2026: Judge Rita Lin rules the Pentagon's measures against Anthropic unlawful in the California case.
  • August 31, 2026: The Department of Defense announces ChatGPT and Grok variants are live on GenAI.mil alongside the already-available Gemini for Government; Claude remains absent.
