Amodei Says AI Backlash Is 'a Crisis of Trust,' Not a Reaction to His Warnings

Responding to investor criticism on X, the Anthropic CEO argued public distrust of AI predates his own statements -- and pointed to Anthropic's unmet promises as the more valid complaint.

What Happened

On August 15, 2026, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei posted a series of responses on X pushing back on criticism from investor Gavin Baker, who had argued on the All-In podcast that Amodei's public warnings about AI risk were fueling a broader backlash against the technology, according to TechCrunch.

Amodei's Argument

"I think it is fundamentally a crisis of trust," Amodei wrote. "I think that ordinary people don't trust companies, governments, or the tech industry." He argued this distrust predates AI and reflects decades of institutional skepticism, calling the current backlash "just the latest iteration" of a longer pattern rather than a direct reaction to statements from him or other AI executives.

Pushing Back, and Conceding a Point

Amodei rejected the idea that his public messaging skews negative, saying his output has been "about equally balanced between risks and benefits," and pointed to his essay "Machines of Loving Grace" as evidence he has tried to articulate a positive vision for the technology. He did concede one criticism as valid: "we haven't yet delivered on our big promises to benefit the world" -- arguing concrete breakthroughs, not marketing language, are what would rebuild trust.

What He Proposed

Amodei called for regulation addressing cybersecurity, biosecurity and alignment risks simultaneously, while avoiding rules that "disadvantage frontier AI companies while advantaging smaller competitors." He also argued for a careful approach to open-weight models that accounts for their specific risks without an outright ban.

Facts vs. Amodei's Own Framing

Everything above is Amodei's stated position, posted on his own platform -- not an independently verified finding. VexlowHQ has not checked claims like "ordinary people don't trust... the tech industry" against survey data, and presents them here as his argument, not established fact.
