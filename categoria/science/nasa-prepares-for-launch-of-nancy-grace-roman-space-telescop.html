NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Launches Successfully

The observatory lifted off August 30, 2026 aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy and is now headed to its deep-space observing post, nearly a million miles from Earth.

Leonardo Beltran·August 26, 2026·Updated September 9, 2026·7 min
Correction: Resolved from Batch #7-20 (classification A, rewritten and verified during a prior audit; it was noindexed only pending a phase-wide approval that no longer applies under the ongoing global audit). Indexed on 2026-09-09.

NASA

What Happened, When, Where

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched successfully on Sunday, August 30, 2026, at 7:26 a.m. EDT (1126 GMT) from Launch Complex-39A at Kennedy Space Center in Florida, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket in its triple-core, reusable-side-booster configuration, according to Space.com's live launch coverage. The telescope separated from the rocket 31 minutes after liftoff.

The Telescope's Mission

Named for Dr. Nancy Grace Roman, NASA's first chief of astronomy, the observatory carries a 2.4-meter primary mirror -- the same size as Hubble's -- paired with a much wider field of view, letting it survey large swaths of sky in single observations rather than Hubble's narrower, deeper looks. Its primary science goals are studying dark energy and conducting a wide census of exoplanets. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the telescope "will provide a vast new atlas of the universe, potentially revealing more than 100,000 new exoplanets." Roman senior scientist Julie McEnery said the mission "will do a survey of our own Milky Way galaxy and find 20 billion stars" -- both are NASA's own statements of the mission's potential, not yet-achieved results.

Timeline

  • Aug 24, 2026: Telescope sealed inside its payload fairing.
  • Aug 25, 2026: Transported to SpaceX's hangar at LC-39A.
  • Aug 28, 2026: Launch Readiness Review completed; mission declared "go."
  • Aug 30, 2026: Launch and separation from the Falcon Heavy.
  • Aug 31, 2026: A 3.5-minute engine burn completes a trajectory correction toward its destination.
  • Sept 1, 2026: The telescope's coronagraph instrument powers on for initial testing.

Where It's Headed

Roman is bound for the Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2 (L2), roughly 930,000 miles from Earth -- the same gravitationally stable vantage point used by the James Webb Space Telescope -- where it is expected to operate for at least five years, with a possible mission extension.

Correcting the Record

This article previously described the telescope as still "preparing" for an August 30 launch. It has since launched successfully; this version reflects the confirmed outcome, exact timeline and on-record NASA quotes in place of the earlier generic, forward-looking language.
